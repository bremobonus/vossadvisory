## 2. Pressure that is real (and lawful)

1. **Regulator complaints.** RIBO can discipline a broker. FSRA can act against an insurer's unfair or deceptive practices. The General Insurance OmbudService (GIO) takes the dispute after Aviva's final response. A brokerage cares about a RIBO file far more than a $110 claim. Complain because the conduct is a problem, and say what you want.
2. **A short, precise demand letter.** One page: the policy wording that lets you cancel in writing, the Nov 5 timestamp, the items you want (confirmation of the end date, reversal of the $110, a refund of unearned premium, written confirmation no further debit), and a deadline of 14 days. Cite the specific clause. Precision reads as pressure; length reads as noise (Rates already dismissed your last letter as too long).
3. **Bank claim** for any debit outside the PAD terms (Payments Canada Rule H1, 90 days, verify), which also gives you a record.
4. **File in Small Claims** (Form 7A) if the demand fails. Costs for a self-represented party are small and mostly filing fees (verify amounts). The cost to the other side is a lawyer's time on a small file.
5. **Offer to settle** under Rule 14 so costs consequences can attach (verify).

## 3. What not to do

- **Do not link a regulator complaint or criminal charge to payment.** "Pay me or I report you" can be treated as extortion in the criminal sense. Say you are filing the complaint, and make your settlement terms a separate matter.
- **Do not claim the $859.28 until the bank statement shows it left.** Your Nov 13 notice says it did. Aviva says it did not. A claim for money you did not lose damages your credibility on everything else.
- **Do not inflate.** Small Claims judges and ombudsmen discount a claim that asks for punitive damages on a $110 dispute.
- **Do not threaten the individual employees.** Blanca Muniz Campos, Harneet Kaur and Shaloo Kakkar acted as employees. The defendants are the companies.

## 4. The weak points the other side will use

1. **Duty to mitigate.** Aviva offered a one-page form. A judge may ask why you did not sign it under reservation. Refusing can reduce or defeat your damages and cost you the "cancelled for non-payment" mark on your record (Dec 21).
2. **The Oct 10 signature.** InsureSign shows you signed documents (thread 1a127d3f27f0a119). If that includes the PAD, "unauthorized debit" must be narrowed to the amount, the date, or the Nov 2 change.
3. **Your Nov 2 policy change.** What changed and who asked? The brokerage will have its notes.
4. **Tone.** 48-hour deadlines, "punitive damages" and the AI remark make the file read as adversarial. A neutral cancellation request would have been harder to refuse.
5. **Your wider record.** The licence suspension (which you disclosed Oct 20) and the pending charges may come up on cross-examination about the policy.
